  derive from Source IP and Timestamp, which CIC-UNSW-NB15's Data.csv does not carry,
  so IF and AE cannot be scored on the target at all. This measures the supervised
  member in isolation and says nothing about the deployed cascade.
  Binary attack-vs-benign on both sides: the two attack taxonomies do not align, so a
  multi-class cross-dataset score would need a category mapping that does not exist.

HOW TO READ THIS
============================================================================
  Compare the three rows of a direction in order. The first-to-second step is the price
  of restricting to the shared columns; the second-to-third step is generalization.
  Attributing the whole drop to generalization would overstate it, which is exactly the
  mistake the middle row exists to prevent.
  Prevalence differs between the two test sets, so precision is not comparable across
  directions at face value. Recall, FPR and AUC are.
  A cross-dataset collapse is a finding, not a failure of the experiment: these are two
  different testbeds with different attack tooling, captured years apart. What it
  establishes is the boundary of the single-dataset claim.
